Spellcheck Suspect Words: two NEW hits from this page --
"destruirémos" (v.21) and "realize" (v.15) -- both researched per the
mandatory three-step check and judged legitimate archaic/period forms,
added to `permitted words.txt`, NOT added to `errors in 1920.txt`:
- "realize" (que esto se realize, v.15): 1886
  (pages_1886/page_0584.png, book page 566/file 584) prints the
  identical "se realize" at the same spot, word-for-word -- strong
  same-lineage period evidence, not a one-off 1920 misprint. WebSearch
  of RAE DLE confirms modern standard Spanish only recognizes
  "realizar"/"realice" (a "realice o realize" mix-up page even exists
  warning against "realize" as an anglicism), but RAE's *current*
  dictionary speaks to today's usage, not 1880s-1920s typesetting, and
  the two-edition match is the more direct evidence for this specific
  translation's own period spelling. The modern LDS Spanish edition
  rewords this verse entirely (no comparable word survives there) and
  the "realiz-" stem has zero hits in all three reference corpora
  (Quijote, Reina-Valera NT 1909, full Reina-Valera Bible) -- both
  checked but uninformative either way, since "realizar" in this sense
  is a later verb not really attested in those older, 17th-century
  register texts regardless of spelling.
- "destruirémos" (diga: Nosotros destruirémos la obra, v.21): WebSearch
  research confirms this accented form is the pre-1999 RAE orthography
  reform convention -- before that reform, the "ui" sequence in verb
  forms like this one was NOT treated as an automatic diphthong, so the
  final-syllable stress took a written accent; the reform later
  reclassified "ui" as a diphthong, which is specifically why modern
  "destruiremos" takes no accent. Since 1920 predates 1999 by decades,
  the accented form is simply period-correct orthography, not an
  error. Corroborated by the modern LDS Spanish edition, which renders
  the same clause as "Destruiremos la obra del Señor" (same word, same
  stem, unaccented per the post-reform rule) -- a direct match once the
  orthography-era difference is accounted for.
